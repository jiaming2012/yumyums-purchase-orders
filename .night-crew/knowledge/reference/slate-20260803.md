## Forks — resolved at planning, not at 3am

### FORK (operator, product) — the two list views have no expressible replication scope · **B-43**

**Put as a user story:** *as a crew member on the truck, when a teammate completes or approves a
checklist, do I need my list to change on its own while I'm looking at it, or is it enough that it
is right when I open the app?*

Card `sync-replication-scope-per-checklist` (20260802, A1) made `scope.checklistId` **mandatory and
singular**. That is exactly right for the fill view — the view `architecture/C-2` names — and it is
**not the view a crew member lands on**. `workflows.html` opens on **My Checklists** (every
submission assigned to this user) and its second tab is **Approvals** (every rejection awaiting
this user). Both are lists over MANY submissions; neither can name a single `checklistId`.

🛑 **OPERATOR DECISION, 2026-08-02 evening: LISTS STAY LIVE — the scope is WIDENED.**

`sync-hard-cutover` admits a **recorded C-2 widening**: `checklists` scoped to
`assigned_to.eq.<userId>` plus a date floor, and the approver-side collections scoped to this
approver, so both list views replicate and update live. This is the closest behaviour to today's —
`sync.js`'s WebSocket already drives `loadMyChecklists()` — and the operator chose to keep it rather
than trade it for a smaller card.

**What this decision costs, stated because it was stated when the decision was taken:**
1. It is a **deliberate widening of ledger T-29 decision 105** (*"replication scope is
   per-open-checklist, never all collections at once; no card may widen it without a recorded
   decision"*). **This slate IS that recorded decision.** The rule is not repealed — it is amended
   to: *per-open-checklist for the fill collections; per-user-with-a-date-floor for the two list
   collections; never all history, never all users.*
2. It **re-opens the per-phone storage bound.** B-42 already recorded that the bound moved from
   *all history* to *opened checklists* and is **improved, not fixed** — nothing evicts. A per-user
   list scope widens it again, which is why **the date floor is not optional and is a `done_when:`
   row**, not a nicety.
3. It makes S1 **meaningfully bigger than one card** — which is what fires the split below.

**Rejected framings, recorded so they are not re-derived:** *fill-view-only* (cheapest, matches C-2
literally, but the crew loses live list updates they have today, and the offline story splits into
two owner classes which E-KR3 is graded against) and *poll-on-focus* (recovers most of the
behaviour without a widening, but adds a second refresh mechanism alongside RxDB's own, which is the
dual-offline-story E-KR3 forbids).
